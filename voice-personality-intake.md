# Understudy intake curriculum (v2, 2026-07-28)

Four modules, ordered by information gain toward earliest usefulness. The
Mini-IPIP 20-item inventory interleaves with module 1. The eight validation
questions are sealed: asked last, never ingested, used only for evaluation.

## 1. Identity & values
Kept from the original McAdams-derived set (highest-information):
- Introduce yourself…
- If a friend called you right now…
- Tell me about where you grew up…
- What was a fork in the road…
- Tell me about a period of your life that was genuinely hard…
- How are you different from the person you were ten years ago…
- What is something you believe that most people…
- What makes you lose respect…
- Describe a time you changed your mind…
- What do you think makes a life well lived…
- When you have to make a big decision…
- What role do you tend to play in a group…
- What do people misunderstand about you…
- What is a flaw of yours…
- What do you want people to say about you…

## 2. Communication situations
1. How do you typically open and close an email? Any phrases you reach for again and again — greetings, sign-offs, the way you get to the point?
2. Someone asks you for something you don't have time for. Say no to them, out loud, the way you actually would — then tell me about a real time you had to.
3. How do you disagree with someone senior to you? And how is that different from how you'd push back on a peer?
4. Walk me through delivering bad news — a project slipping, a mistake that was yours. What do you say first, and what do you refuse to say?
5. What does your praise sound like? Say what you'd actually tell someone who just did genuinely great work.
6. How do you chase someone who owes you something — a reply, a decision, a deliverable — without souring the relationship? Give me the actual words.
7. How does your tone shift between a close colleague, your boss, and a stranger you're writing to cold? Show me the same request in each register if you can.
8. How do you ask for help when you're genuinely stuck? What do you say, and what do you make sure to include?
9. How do you apologize when you got something wrong? Talk me through a real one if you're willing.
10. What communication habits in other people annoy you — in email, in meetings, in chat — and what do you do instead?
11. 📎 Upload two or three real emails you're proud of — ones where the tone came out exactly right. Demonstrated style beats described style. (Drop the files here or paste the text; names and addresses get redacted at ingest.)
12. 📎 Upload something formal you've written — a proposal, a report, a letter that had to be official.
13. 📎 Upload something casual — a chat thread, an informal note, anything written in your relaxed voice.

## 3. Work & craft
Kept: Explain what you do for work… · Tell me about a project or piece of work you consider your best… · Describe a failure at work… · What would you work on if money… · What does ambition mean to you…
New:
1. Describe an ordinary working day, start to finish — the real one, not the ideal one. Where does the time actually go?
2. What decisions do you make over and over in your work? Pick one and walk me through how you actually make it.
3. Which parts of your job do you love, and which do you quietly dread? Be specific about both.
4. Who do you answer to, and who answers to you? How do those relationships actually work day to day — not the org chart, the reality.
5. What does genuinely good work look like in your field, in your own words? What separates it from merely acceptable work?
6. What are you working on right now? What state is it in, and what's blocking it?
7. When you look at someone else's work in your field, what do you check first to judge whether they're good?

## 4. Interests & passions
Kept: What is a strong opinion you hold about food, music…
New:
1. What do you read, watch, or follow regularly? What do you never miss, and why that?
2. What topic could you happily talk about for an hour with zero preparation? Prove it — give me the first five minutes.
3. What are you learning right now, or itching to learn next? What's drawing you to it?
4. What did you love as a teenager that you still care about today? What survived and what didn't?
5. What do you collect, practice, or tinker with? What does the time you sink into it give you back?
6. What's a take you hold about your field or a hobby that gets pushback? Make the case for it.
7. If work vanished tomorrow, what would fill your weeks within a month? Be honest, not aspirational.

## Validation (sealed, unchanged)
Eight questions, quarantined by id and content hash from every ingestion path.
